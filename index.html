Ganzflicker

Put the headset on, close your eyes, and let a full-field flicker shine through your eyelids. At the right rate, most people start seeing colors, geometric patterns, and sometimes whole scenes.

Read this first

Flashing light in the 3–30 Hz range can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy, including people who have never had one before. Don't use this if you or close family have epilepsy or seizures, if you've had migraines triggered by light, or if you're tired, drunk, or high.

Stop right away if you feel dizzy, nauseous, get a headache, notice muscle twitching, or feel confused. Pressing any controller button or tapping the screen stops the flicker instantly. Have someone nearby the first time.

      Sound

      Steady drumming around 4–5 beats per second is the traditional rhythm for journeying. Locked to the flashes, the drum strikes on every flash or every second or third one, whichever lands closest to your tempo. With a gentle wake-up ending, the drum plays the traditional callback: slow beats, then a fast roll to bring you back.

      Tones follow the flicker rate, including journeys and drift. Binaural beats play a slightly different pitch in each ear; the difference equals the flicker rate.

          Session

          Pattern

          A glowing sun symbol (a circle with a dot in the middle) and a soft tone guide your breathing: the dot swells until the circle fills with light as you breathe in, holds while you hold, and shrinks back as you breathe out. A small chime marks each new breath. Then the flicker fades in while the guide fades away over two more breaths.

          Checking for VR support…

          The flicker starts dim and reaches full brightness after 4 seconds. Eyes-open sessions hit harder than closed-eye ones, so start around 30% brightness. The headset's refresh rate limits how exactly a rate can be hit.

          Breath

          Breath journey

          This is a gentle practice, so there's no safety check. Sit comfortably with your back straight, or lie down.

          Safety check

          Don't do this if you have heart disease, angina, high blood pressure or a past heart attack; glaucoma or retinal detachment; osteoporosis; epilepsy or any seizure disorder; a family history of aneurysm; a recent injury or surgery; or a history of psychosis, panic attacks or severe mental illness. Don't do it while pregnant or breastfeeding. Ask a doctor first if you take regular medication or have asthma.

          Fast breathing often causes tingling, light-headedness or cramping in the hands and face. Slowing down eases it. Stop and breathe normally if you feel chest pain, faintness or overwhelming distress. Breath holds after fast breathing can make you pass out, so always sit or lie down, and never do this standing, driving, in a bath or near water. It can bring up strong emotions, so have someone sit with you, and consider a trained facilitator, especially the first time.

          The glowing symbol and an airy sound pace your breathing for the first 4 minutes, then fade so you can find your own rhythm.

          Breath holds

          Fast breathing and flashing light are both used in hospitals to trigger seizures during brain tests, so they are never combined here. The flicker starts only after 7 minutes of normal breathing, at 10 Hz and low brightness.
